Buy the Dip Strategy on Bitcoin: Takashi Kotegawa's 25-Day Moving Average Rule Gave 1 Trade in 3 Years

13 Nov 2026 · Backtest Bharat

The buy the dip strategy built on Takashi Kotegawa's 25-day moving average number traded once on Binance BTC/USDT from Nov 2023 to Nov 2026 and returned +11.6% with a 0.1% fee per side. Simply holding made +105.4%.

A Japanese trader known online as BNF once wrote down the number he used for buying falling stocks: 20% below the 25-day moving average. (In India BNF usually means Bank Nifty Futures. Here it is one man's nickname.) On 3 years of Bitcoin it gave one buy signal in 1,096 days.

Key takeaways
  • The buy the dip rule: buy when the daily close is 20% or more below the 25-day moving average, and sell at the close three days later.
  • On Binance BTC/USDT, Nov 2023 to Nov 2026 (0.1% fee), it traded once. ₹1 lakh became ₹1,11,600. Simply holding Bitcoin made it ₹2,05,400.
  • After India's 30% crypto tax, the rule kept ₹1,08,100 and holding kept ₹1,73,800. One trade is too few to give a win rate.

Who is BNF (Takashi Kotegawa)?

  • BNF is the name he used on 2channel, a Japanese message board. A 2008 newspaper profile says it comes from the initials of Victor Niederhoffer, a US investor he admired. It has nothing to do with Bank Nifty
  • Bloomberg reported his real name, Takashi Kotegawa, in December 2005
  • He started trading shares online in 2000 with ¥1.6 million of savings
  • On Dec 8, 2005 a broker meant to sell 1 J-Com share at ¥610,000 and instead offered 610,000 shares at ¥1. Kotegawa, then 27, bought 7,100 shares and made more than ¥2 billion
  • He told Bloomberg: "I was so scared to see massive selling. I didn't know what was happening."
  • In February 2008 a reporter saw the total on his trading screen: ¥19,465,864,683

▶ English: his magazine interviews and his own 2004 message-board posts on the 25-day rule. Source: Guanxinated (YouTube, 2021)

Newer net worth figures you see online, in dollars or in rupees, have no source we could trace. The 2008 number above is the last one we found in a newspaper interview.

The 4 rules we tested

In a post dated Oct 3, 2004, Kotegawa wrote that the key number for short-term dip buying is the deviation rate: how far the price is from its 25-day moving average.

  • In the falling markets of 2001 and 2002, he wanted a stock at least 20% below its 25-day average, and felt safe buying only at 35% or more
  • From late 2003, he wrote, stocks bounced after only a 15% gap, and there were few chances to buy this way at all
  • In a post dated Nov 17, 2004, he described the rebounds as lasting one or two nights

We turned that into a fixed rule a computer can follow.

1
Draw the average25-day simple average of daily closing prices
2
Measure the gapDeviation = (close ÷ 25-day average − 1) × 100
3
Buy the dipIf the day closes 20% or more below the average, buy at that close
4
Sell on day threeSell at the close of the third day after buying, win or lose

His numbers were for Japanese stocks in 2001 and 2002. He used them as a first filter and changed them with the market. Using 20% as a fixed rule on Bitcoin is our experiment, not his method.

His own warning, from a post dated Nov 17, 2004. Source: archived 2channel post, our translation

On Binance the daily candle closes at 00:00 UTC, which is 5:30 AM in India. That is the "close" in this test.

What if you buy smaller dips?

Waiting 3 years for one signal is hard. The usual answer is to lower the bar. Kotegawa himself wrote about a 15% gap in stronger markets, so we tested 15% and 10% too.

The 10% version won 10 of its 17 trades and still made only +4.3% before tax. Its average win was +4.36% and its average loss was -5.23%. Small dips often kept falling for three more days.

After India's 30% crypto tax

One winning trade, taxed once

  • Section 194 of the Income-tax Act, 2025 (Table, item 4) puts a 30% rate on "any income from the transfer of any virtual digital asset"
  • The same item says no "set off of any loss" is allowed, and that the loss "shall not be allowed to be carried forward"
  • The table gives one rate. It has no separate rate for a 3-day trade and a 3-year holding

The actual rule: 30% rate, and losses cannot be set off or carried forward. Source: Income Tax Department, Govt of India

With one winning trade the tax is simple. Out of about ₹11,600 of profit, about ₹3,500 goes as tax and ₹8,100 stays.

The 10% version shows the harder side of the rule. Its 10 winners are taxed one by one and its 7 losers give no relief, so a +4.3% result becomes -7.8%. The 9 20 EMA test showed the same effect with more trades.

1% TDS on each sale

The Income Tax Department's guide on virtual digital assets says the person paying for a crypto sale must deduct tax at 1% of the amount. When the sale goes through an exchange that pays the seller directly, the exchange deducts it.

The department also says that when the tax paid, including tax deducted at source, is more than the tax due, the excess can be claimed as a refund by filing the return of income. Until then that 1% is not in the trading account.

Our backtest does not subtract TDS. With one sale in three years the effect is small. Check how your own exchange applies it.

FAQ

Does the buy the dip strategy work on Bitcoin?

On Binance BTC/USDT from Nov 2023 to Nov 2026, buying a close 20% or more below the 25-day average and selling three days later traded once and returned +11.6% after a 0.1% fee. Holding returned +105.4%. One trade is too few to say it works. Past results do not guarantee future returns.

What is the win rate of this buy the dip rule?

On Binance BTC/USDT from Nov 2023 to Nov 2026, with a 0.1% fee per side, it made 1 trade, so a win rate would mean nothing. On all Binance data since Sep 2017 the same rule made 24 trades and 18 were profitable (75.0%), yet it earned far less than holding.

What is the moving average deviation rate?

It is how far the price is from its moving average, in percent: (close ÷ 25-day average − 1) × 100. On Feb 5, 2026 Bitcoin closed 27.6% below its 25-day average. That was the only close 20% or more below it in the 3-year window.

Who is BNF? Is it Bank Nifty?

Not in this post. In India BNF usually means Bank Nifty Futures. Here BNF is the online nickname of Takashi Kotegawa, a Japanese stock trader who became known after the J-Com trade of December 2005.

Does the Takashi Kotegawa strategy still work?

He gave the 25-day deviation numbers for Japanese stocks in 2001 and 2002, and wrote in 2004 that he could not recommend buying on the deviation rate alone. In a 2008 interview he again said the deviation rate alone is not enough. Our Bitcoin test is a fixed-rule experiment, not his method.

What is Takashi Kotegawa's net worth?

The last figure we could trace is from a February 2008 newspaper interview, where the reporter saw ¥19,465,864,683 (about ¥19.5 billion) on his trading screen. We found no reliable source for newer figures, so we do not quote a current number or a rupee value.

How does India's 30% crypto tax change the result?

On Binance BTC/USDT from Nov 2023 to Nov 2026, with a 0.1% fee per side and 30% tax on the one winning trade, ₹1 lakh became ₹1,08,100 instead of ₹1,11,600. Versions that trade more often did worse: buying 10% dips went from +4.3% before tax to -7.8% after tax.

Method and sources

  • Data: Binance BTC/USDT daily candles (00:00 UTC), Nov 10, 2023 to Nov 9, 2026. Fee 0.1% per side, long only, spot, no leverage, no slippage, full capital on each trade
  • Rule: 25-day simple average of closes, including the signal day. Buy at the close when the close is 20% or more below the average. Sell at the close three days later. One position at a time, no stop-loss. Max drawdown is measured on daily closes
  • A second, separately written calculation on freshly downloaded data gave the same trades and returns
  • Side check: Sep 10, 2017 (the first day Binance data gives a 25-day average) to Nov 9, 2026, same rule and costs
  • Tax model: 30% of each winning trade's profit deducted at exit, losses not set off (Income-tax Act, 2025, Section 194 Table item 4). Simplified: only the 30% rate is modelled. Any surcharge or cess and the TDS cash lock are not included. Check with a chartered accountant
  • Takashi Kotegawa: Bloomberg report in The Globe and Mail, Dec 19, 2005 (name, age, 7,100 shares, profit, quote); ZAKZAK profile, Feb 7, 2008 and ZAKZAK interview, Jan 24, 2008 (archived, in Japanese: nickname, ¥1.6 million start, screen total, 2008 remark on the deviation rate)
