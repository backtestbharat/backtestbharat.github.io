1 Percent TDS on Crypto in India: What 1, 31 and 451 Sales Lock Up From ₹1 Lakh, and Is It Refundable?

13 Nov 2026 · Updated 14 Nov 2026 · Backtest Bharat

India deducts 1% TDS every time you sell crypto. One percent sounds too small to matter.

We counted it on three real backtests with ₹1 lakh. One sale had ₹2,054 deducted. 451 sales had ₹2,56,800 deducted, about four times the tax that was actually owed.

Key takeaways
  • 1% TDS is taken from the sale amount on every crypto sale, win or lose. It counts as tax already paid when you file your return.
  • With 1 sale in 3 years (HODL), TDS was ₹2,054 against a tax bill of ₹31,600. It changed nothing.
  • With 451 sales (volatility breakout), ₹2,56,800 was deducted against ₹62,300 of tax. In FY 2025-26 the account fell from ₹91,000 to ₹17,200 while ₹56,200 waited as a refund claim.

What is the 1% TDS on crypto?

TDS means tax deducted at source. The Income Tax Department's own guide on crypto (it calls crypto a virtual digital asset, VDA) says this:

  • Tax is deducted "at the rate of 1% of the consideration". Consideration is the sale amount, not the profit
  • When you sell through an exchange and the exchange pays you directly, the exchange deducts it
  • For intraday trading, "the tax shall be deducted every time a transaction is squared-off"
  • No TDS if the total for the financial year does not exceed ₹10,000 (₹50,000 when the payer is a "specified person" as the guide defines it)
  • If the seller has not given a PAN, the rate is 20%

The rule in the department's words: 1% of the sale amount, every time a trade is closed. Source: Income Tax Department, Govt of India

The guide uses the old section number, 194S. For tax year 2026-27 the department's TDS rate chart lists the same 1% and the same ₹10,000 and ₹50,000 limits under Section 393(1), Table S. No. 8(vi) of the Income-tax Act, 2025.

How we counted it: 4 rules

1
Take 1% from every sale1% of the sale amount leaves the trading account at each sale, win or lose
2
Count the 30% tax as before30% of the profit on each winning sale. Losing sales give no relief
3
Treat TDS as tax already paidInside one financial year (1 April to 31 March), TDS is set against that year's tax. Extra tax is paid only when the tax is bigger
4
Give back the extra on 1 AprilIf the year's TDS is more than the year's tax, the difference returns to the account on 1 April

Rule 4 is the best case. A real refund comes later, only after the return is filed. So we also show the other extreme: no refund at all during the 3 years.

Real life sits somewhere between the two.

On the losing day no tax is due, but ₹249 still leaves the account. On the winning day the TDS (₹249) is bigger than the tax on that profit (₹191).

TDS follows the size of the sale. Tax follows the size of the profit. A strategy with many sales and thin profits pays TDS much faster than it creates tax.

HODL: no change. The ₹2,054 was simply the first part of the ₹31,600 tax.

Read the breakout row carefully. Account plus refund due is ₹62,600, more than the ₹38,800 in the no-TDS test. TDS did not earn anything. It pulled money out of a losing strategy, so that money could not be traded, lost, or taxed again. Tax owed fell from ₹86,000 to ₹62,300 for the same reason.

Why TDS runs ahead of the 30% tax

  • Income from the transfer of a virtual digital asset is taxed at 30%
  • A loss cannot be set off against other income, and cannot be carried forward to later years
  • This rule is Section 194 (Table, item 4) of the Income-tax Act, 2025

The tax rule: 30% rate, and losses cannot be set off or carried forward. Source: Income Tax Department, Govt of India

The breakout made 205 winning sales and 246 losing sales. Tax is counted only on the 205. TDS is taken on all 451.

The average winning sale made +1.67%. 30% of that is about 0.5% of the sale amount. TDS is 1% of the sale amount, on winners and losers alike.

So for a high-frequency strategy, TDS is not a small slice of the tax. It is several times the tax. Here it was 4.1 times.

One real year: FY 2025-26

On 1 April 2025 the breakout account held ₹91,000. Over the next 12 months it sold 147 times and ₹69,900 of TDS was deducted. The tax owed on that year's winning sales was ₹13,700.

On 31 March 2026 the account showed ₹17,200, down 81%. But ₹56,200 was due back as a refund. Counting that, the year's real loss was 19.3%.

The loss on paper is one problem. The bigger one is practical: a strategy needs capital to trade, and most of it was no longer in the account.

The maths is simple. Each full sale leaves 99% in the account. After 12 full sales 11.4% is held as TDS credit, after 52 it is 40.7%, and after 147 it is 77.2%.

The real year matched that closely: ₹69,900 is 76.9% of the ₹91,000 the year started with.

Is TDS on crypto refundable?

Here is what the Income Tax Department's public pages say.

  • You can check the TDS credit in Form 168 in your e-filing account and "claim the credit of the tax" in your return of income. The claim "should be strictly as per the TDS credit being reflected in Form 168"
  • When the tax paid, including tax deducted at source, "is more than the required amount", the taxpayer "will be eligible to claim refund of the excess tax paid"
  • The refund can be claimed only by filing the return of income
  • Refund processing "starts only after the return is e-verified", and "Usually, it takes 4-5 weeks for the refund to be credited"

So the money is not lost. But it is away from the trading account from the day of each sale until the refund arrives. In our FY 2025-26 example that was ₹56,200 out of ₹91,000.

FAQ

Is the 1% TDS on crypto refundable?

The Income Tax Department says a taxpayer whose tax paid, including tax deducted at source, is more than the required amount can claim a refund of the excess, and that the refund is claimed by filing the return of income. In our 451-sale test, ₹56,200 was due back for FY 2025-26.

How do I claim a crypto TDS refund?

According to the Income Tax Department, the TDS credit shows in Form 168 in your e-filing account and you claim that credit in your return of income. Refund processing starts only after the return is e-verified and usually takes 4-5 weeks.

Is TDS deducted when I sell crypto at a loss?

The rule is 1% of the consideration, which is the sale amount, so it does not depend on profit. In our test, a losing sale of ₹24,919 on Nov 2, 2026 still had ₹249 deducted.

What is the TDS rate on crypto in India?

1% of the sale consideration. The department's guide says no TDS is deducted if the total for the financial year does not exceed ₹10,000 (₹50,000 where the payer is a specified person), and that the rate is 20% if the seller has not given a PAN.

Is TDS deducted on every intraday crypto trade?

The department's guide says that where a person does intraday trading in cryptocurrencies, the tax is deducted every time a transaction is squared off. In our test, 147 sales in FY 2025-26 had ₹69,900 deducted from an account that started the year at ₹91,000.

Is the 1% TDS an extra tax on top of the 30% crypto tax?

TDS is tax collected at the time of the sale, and you claim it as credit in your return. In our HODL test the ₹2,054 of TDS was the first part of the ₹31,600 tax, so nothing extra was paid.

How much TDS is deducted on a ₹1 lakh crypto sale?

1% of ₹1,00,000 is ₹1,000. If the same ₹1 lakh is sold 12 times in one financial year, about 11.4% of it ends up held as TDS credit before any refund.
